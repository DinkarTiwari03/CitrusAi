Disease: Citrus Canker

Citrus canker is a bacterial disease that affects citrus leaves, stems and fruit. It is commonly associated with the bacterium Xanthomonas citri.

Symptoms:
Citrus canker produces raised, corky or crater-like lesions. Lesions may have yellow halos around them. Multiple lesions can occur across the leaf surface. Severe infection can cause premature leaf and fruit drop.

Disease spread:
The disease can spread through wind-driven rain, splashing water, infected plant material and contaminated tools. Young leaves and actively growing tissues can be particularly susceptible.

Management:
Remove and properly dispose of severely infected plant material where recommended. Maintain orchard sanitation. Avoid unnecessary movement of contaminated plant material between plants. Prune appropriately to improve canopy conditions when appropriate.

Prevention:
Use healthy planting material. Monitor plants regularly for new lesions. Reduce practices that cause unnecessary movement of contaminated water or plant material.

Important:
Chemical control should follow locally approved agricultural recommendations and product labels. Disease management should consider local regulations and environmental conditions.
